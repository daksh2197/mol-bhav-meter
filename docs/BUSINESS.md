# How this could pay for itself

The meter is free and stays free. This page is the honest version of how a free calculator turns into money, what each path needs to be true, and what I don't know yet.

No market-size numbers here. I haven't measured the market, so I'm not going to make one up.

## Who it's for

People who buy from vendors and resell, and who do the buying by phone or in person:

- Small online sellers sourcing from wholesalers (their own store, Etsy, Amazon, Instagram shops).
- Family shops where one person sells and another person buys.
- Resellers and flippers pricing a lot before they bid.

The moment it's for: a price has just been said out loud and you have seconds to answer.

## What's free forever

The calculator, the verdict, the counter-offer, sharing and offline use. That's the reason anyone finds the tool, and charging for it would kill the one thing it's good at: being opened fast, by someone who has never heard of me.

## Three ways it can earn

### 1. The Vendor Desk kit (digital download)

The meter judges one quote. The kit runs the whole buying desk: a vendor tracker, a call log, a price guide that sets walk-away costs for a full catalogue, and a call script. A spreadsheet plus a short PDF.

- **Price to test:** $9, or ₹499 in India.
- **What has to be true:** people who use the free meter also want the system around it.
- **Cost to deliver:** near zero. It's a file.
- **Why first:** it can be built in a weekend and needs no new code.

### 2. Pro, a one-time unlock

Save vendors, compare quotes side by side, keep a history of what each vendor quoted last time, export a one-page deal sheet. Everything still stored on the device.

- **Price to test:** $19 once. Not a subscription. Sourcing is seasonal, and nobody wants a monthly bill for a calculator.
- **What has to be true:** people come back for a second and third quote. If they only ever use it once, Pro has no buyer.
- **Cost to deliver:** a few days of building, payment through a hosted checkout link.

### 3. Embedded for wholesalers (business-to-business)

A wholesaler or trade marketplace puts "check your margin" next to its own prices. A buyer who can see their profit orders faster. The meter already has an embed mode (`?embed=1`) for exactly this.

- **Price to test:** a monthly licence per site.
- **What has to be true:** the wholesaler believes it lifts orders. That needs one pilot with real before-and-after numbers.
- **Cost to deliver:** mostly sales time and support, not code.
- **Why last:** it's the biggest cheque and the slowest yes.

## What it costs to run

| Thing | Cost |

| Hosting (GitHub Pages) | $0 |

There is no monthly bill to outrun. The break-even is the first sale.

## The arithmetic of a first $500

| Path | Price | Sales needed |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Vendor Desk kit | $9 | 56 |
| Pro unlock | $19 | 27 |
| Mix: 30 kits + 13 Pro | | 43 |

Code doesn't produce those sales. Distribution does. Fifty-six strangers have to see the meter, try it, and want more. That's the real work, and it's the part I'm testing first.

## The one-week demand test (before building anything paid)

1. Ship the free meter.
2. Add a "Get the Vendor Desk kit" link that goes to a waitlist form. Nothing to buy yet.
3. Put the meter in front of sellers: three short videos of real quotes going through it, plus posts in seller communities where sharing tools is welcome.
4. Count three numbers: visits, people who typed a quote, waitlist sign-ups.

**Go:** 25 or more sign-ups in a week. Build the kit and email them.
**No-go:** fewer than 5. The tool is fine, the offer is wrong. Change the offer, not the code.

## What I don't know yet

- Whether sellers will pay for a system around the meter, or just use the free part and leave.
- Which side pays better: the seller ($9 at a time) or the wholesaler (one bigger contract).
- Whether Hindi matters more than new features. My guess is yes. A guess is not data.

## Ground rules

- No ads and no selling data. The meter doesn't collect any, so there's nothing to sell.
- Anything paid has a refund button.
- Tax rates are typed by the user. The meter is a calculator and says so.
